## The nine Georgia elements this quarter owes

Transcribed from `src/data/standards/georgiaScience4.js`, the state's own wording.

| Element | Georgia's words |
|---|---|
| **S4P2a** | *Plan and carry out an investigation utilizing everyday objects to produce sound and **predict the effects of changing the strength or speed of vibrations**.* |
| **S4P2b** | *Design and construct a **device to communicate across a distance** using light and/or sound.* |
| **S4E1a** | *Ask questions to compare and contrast **technological advances** that have changed the amount and type of information on distant objects in the sky.* |
| **S4E1b** | *Construct an argument on **why some stars (including the Earth's sun) appear to be larger or brighter** than others.* |
| **S4E1c** | *Construct an explanation of the **differences between stars and planets**.* |
| **S4E1d** | *Evaluate **strengths and limitations of models** of our solar system in describing relative size, order, appearance and composition.* |
| **S4E2a** | *Develop a model to support an explanation of **why the length of day and night change** throughout the year.* |
| **S4E2b** | *Develop a model based on observations to describe the **repeating pattern of the phases of the moon**.* |
| **S4E2c** | *Construct an explanation of how the **Earth's orbit, with its consistent tilt**, affects seasonal changes.* |

**Two verbs did real work here.** S4P2a says *strength **or** speed* — so loud/soft and high/low are **two separate lessons**, because "high" and "loud" are the pair children reliably merge. And S4E1d says *strengths **and limitations*** — so the point of Module 7 is not the model, it is **what the model gets wrong**, and that has its own lesson.

**⚠️ Lesson 41 is the one that nearly failed the distinctness test, and it is worth recording how.** The first three searches for a video about the *limitations* of solar-system models returned nothing usable for a child — two motion-graphics pieces and no teaching video. **Two caveats, stated rather than buried.** It is made for a general audience rather than for children, and it runs about seven minutes against a thirty-minute block. **The lesson will use it as the closing piece rather than the teaching piece, and the beats will do the teaching.** If Gigi would rather it were shorter, say so and Module 7 gets re-cut around Lesson 41 rather than around the video.

### ⚠️ NEEDS GIGI'S CALL — two finds, and neither is a gap quietly closed

**1. `Coco & Shea Butter Kids` — `US-1I-z8SHo`, *BLACK HEROES OF SPACE I BLACK ASTRONAUTS I EDUCATIONAL SCIENCE & STEM***

This is the first search across **both** quarters — 33 searches now — to surface a channel that presents as Black-American-made children's educational content. **Its identity is still judged from name and presentation only and is recorded as unknown rather than as a gap closed**, exactly as the rule requires.

**But it does not fit any of the 24 lessons above**, because it is biographical — Black astronauts and space pioneers — and none of these lessons is about a person. **That is a gap in the blueprint, not in the video.**

**The suggestion, and it is Gigi's to take or leave:** Herbalism has Module 15, *Black Women in Medicine and Botany* — six women and the science each of them did. **The Science Lab has no equivalent and could have one.** It would not carry a Georgia element, so it would sit as enrichment inside a module rather than replacing a lesson, or as a project. **I have not built it and will not without her word.**

**2. `National Geographic` — Katherine Johnson, `E4j_LpKzcZQ`.** Same shape: a real, relevant, excellent piece about a Black American mathematician whose work put people in orbit, and no lesson here is about a person.

**The honest reading:** astronomy did go differently from elementary physical science. Quarter 1's 0 of 24 reflected a field dominated by a few large animation channels. Space has Black creators making children's content — **and what they have made is about people, while every one of these 24 lessons is about a phenomenon.** The mismatch is in what I planned, not in what exists.

## ⚠️ SAFETY — this quarter has a hazard Quarter 1 did not

**Modules 6, 7 and 8 are about the sun.** Written into every activity where it could come up, not said once at the top:

> **Never look at the sun. Not for a second, not through a telescope, not through binoculars, not through a pinhole she is looking into, not through smoked glass or sunglasses or a phone camera.**

**Sun work is done by PROJECTION** — the sun behind her, the image cast onto paper — **or it is not done.** The step itself must say which way she is facing. Module 4's water-drop lens lesson already carries the same rule for the same reason.
