## 1. The Herbalist's Journal — her Journal tab

Four kinds of entry, each with its own rotating prompt:

| Kind | What it asks for | Earns at |
|---|---|---|
| 👀 What I noticed | Anything she saw today | 8 words |
| 🌿 A plant I looked at | A structured observation of one plant | 12 words |
| 🌼 Pressed flower log | What she picked, where, and when | 8 words |
| ✍️ A longer piece | A full paragraph or more | 40 words |

**Nothing is graded and nothing is corrected.** No spellcheck scolding, no red underline, no "try again." A child who learns that writing is a thing you get marked down for writes less.

**Petals are paid for effort, never for quality.** The amount is identical whether the entry is beautiful or barely coherent. The moment an app pays more for "better" writing, it has taught a nine-year-old to write for the machine.

**Every prompt points at something she can observe**, not something she has to invent. "Pick one leaf. Is the edge smooth, toothed, or lobed?" has an answer in the garden — she can go and get it. "Write about a time you felt brave" is where a child sits for ten minutes and decides she doesn't like writing.

No prompt anywhere asks what a plant is *for* or what it treats. That rule holds at the source, not just at the check.

You can read every entry in **Grown-Up Corner → Her journal**, and print it for a portfolio. It's read-only from your side, deliberately — the moment she discovers a grown-up rewrites her journal, it stops being hers.

## 2. Notes from Gigi and Mom — on her Home screen

Two senders. **You choose who it's from on each note**, because you both write from the same laptop — otherwise every note her mother writes arrives signed Gigi.

- Written in **Grown-Up Corner → Write to her**
- One line plus a one-tap sticker; the feature that gets used is the one that takes ten seconds
- They sit **above** Dr. Marigold on her Home screen. A note from a real person who knows her outranks anything the software has to say.
- You see "she has read this" and nothing more — no timestamps, no view counts

**Nothing here is auto-generated.** Every word she reads was typed by one of you. A "Great job!" produced by software is worth nothing the moment a child works out where it came from, and children work that out fast.

## 3. Today — her school day, with a bell

A real class bell, synthesized in the browser so there's no sound file to go missing when the folder is copied to her laptop.

**The bell has an on switch, and it has to.** Browsers refuse to play sound until someone interacts with the page — that's why a tab left open overnight can't start ringing at you. She presses "Turn the bell on" each morning; that press is the permission, and it rings once so she hears it.

**The day:** 9:00 AM to 1:55 PM, **240 minutes of instruction**, breaks excluded.

| Time | Block | |
|---|---|---|
| 9:00 | 🌅 Morning Circle | reads your notes, looks at the day |
| 9:15 | 🔢 Mathematics | Khan |
| 9:45 | 📖 Reading | Khan |
| 10:15 | 🍎 Snack & Move | break |
| 10:35 | ✏️ Language Arts & Writing | Khan |
| 11:05 | 🌿 Herbalism & Botany | her course |
| 11:45 | 🥣 Lunch | break |
| 12:20 | 🔬 Science | Khan |
| 12:50 | 🌍 Social Studies | Khan |
| 13:10 | 📓 Journal & Quiet Reading | |
| 13:40 | 🎵 Singing & Movement | |

Every block is editable in **Grown-Up Corner → Her day** — rename, move, lengthen, delete, add your own. Overlaps get a warning, not a refusal; sometimes two things genuinely do happen at once.

**It never nags.** A block she hasn't ticked is simply not ticked. No red, no "you are behind," no overdue count. The day is a plan, not a debt.

## 4. Reset is now safe

**Resetting the Check-In no longer touches her journal, your notes, or her attendance.**

That button exists so the assessment can be retaken. Her writing cannot be regenerated by taking a test again, and neither can a note you wrote her — wiping the only irreplaceable things in the database to fix the most replaceable one was wrong. The confirmation screen now spells out exactly what stays.

## 5. Two new checks, and what they caught

**check-hooks** — caught a real crash before it shipped. I'd written the Today card to read the schedule, bail out early when the day was empty, and *then* call another hook. React counts hooks by order, so that component would have crashed the first time a grown-up added a block to an empty timetable. Every other check passed it: the imports resolved, the brackets balanced. I put the bug back to confirm the guard catches it.

**check-schedule** — caught me claiming four hours and building three hours twenty. Breaks had quietly been counted toward the total. That number could end up on a state form, so it now has to be instruction only, and the check enforces it. It also verifies all five Khan core subjects actually have a block — a subject agreed in a planning document and never put on the timetable never happens.
