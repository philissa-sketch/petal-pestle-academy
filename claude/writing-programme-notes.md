## 1. The collision, and how it resolves

There is a **locked decision** in this app: *nothing in the Journal is graded or corrected* — because a child who learns that writing is a thing you get marked down for writes less.

Teaching writing **in** the Journal is not the same as grading it, and the resolution is clean:

| | |
|---|---|
| **The Journal** | a short writing or grammar **mini-lesson**, then she writes. **Still never graded, never corrected.** Petals for effort, as now. |
| **Book reports & research papers** | the **graded** writing. Separate pieces, with a rubric, in the Gradebook and on the transcript. |

So the daily practice stays safe and the record still gets real evidence. **The graded thing is a finished piece she chose to make, not the private page she writes on every day.**

## 2. "A lesson based on the scores she received already" — with one honest caveat

Her Writing Strategies is **2.45** and her Grammar & Usage is **2.15** — but **both are ceilings, not measurements.** The easiest question she was ever asked was 2.5 in Writing and 2.3 in Grammar, so the test never looked below where it placed her. She is not re-taking the Check-In.

**So the lessons must start below the ceiling, not at it.** We know the test never probed under 2.3; we do not know she is at 2.3. Starting at the beginning costs a few easy weeks and risks nothing. Starting at 2.45 risks building a year she cannot read.

**What her transcript does still tell us, and it is usable:**

- Grammar & Usage **2.15** → Khan Grammar **Unit 1, Parts of speech: the noun**, which is already wired up and confirmed.
- Writing Strategies **2.45** → no Khan course teaches elementary writing at all; this is why it moves to the Journal.
- **10 of her 13 Reading and Vocabulary questions were read aloud to her.** Her Reading 3.46 and Vocabulary 2.91 are *listening* scores. Her independent reading is lower than both — which matters enormously for book reports, where she has to read the book herself.

## 3. The shape to build

### Daily Journal mini-lesson

Five minutes at the top of the Journal block, then she writes as she does now.

- A **rotation** rather than a course: one grammar point or one writing move per day, at a 2.5 reading level.
- Grammar points track Khan Grammar Units 1–7 so the two reinforce rather than diverge.
- Writing moves: a strong opening · one idea per paragraph · showing instead of telling · joining two sentences · a closing that does not say "in conclusion".
- **Nothing she writes afterwards is marked.** The mini-lesson is the teaching; the writing is the practice.

### Book reports

- A short, repeatable frame she can fill: what happened · a character worth talking about · a part she would change · would she pass it to a friend.
- **Read-aloud is allowed and recorded**, exactly as the Check-In records it, because her independent reading is the constraint and hiding that would make the record wrong.
- Graded on a rubric she can see before she starts.

### Research papers

- One per quarter, tied to something she already cares about: an herb, a plant family, a Black American herbalist, a body system.
- Taught as a sequence — **choose a question · find two sources · take notes in her own words · write · check** — not assigned as a finished expectation.
- The first one should be short enough to finish. A research paper a nine-year-old abandons teaches her she cannot write one.
