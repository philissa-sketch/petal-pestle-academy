The 72 writing mini-lessons, in the order she meets them

Petal & Pestle Academy · v3.40 · five minutes at the top of the Journal · never graded, never corrected

What changed. Every lesson now shows the answer, and a lesson may only ask for what it gave her — the build fails otherwise. Every lesson is measured for reading level against a cap that climbs across the year, starting where her Grammar & Usage actually measured (2.15) rather than at her age. And the sentence work runs first, in order, uninterrupted.

How to read this. She meets them in order, one per day she writes in her journal — three days a week means three lessons. After all 72 it starts again as review.
Stage 1 Building a sentence lessons 1–24 · reading cap 2.6
1
A sentence tells one whole thing one right answerKhan unit 7
A sentence tells one whole thing. It can stand on its own.
The seed grew. That is a whole thing.
Try it: Is “under the leaf” a whole thing?
Answer: No. It is not a whole thing.
2
The naming part one right answerKhan unit 7
Every sentence has a naming part. It says who or what.
The bee landed. The bee is the naming part.
Try it: Say the naming part: The rain stopped.
Answer: The rain is the naming part.
3
The doing part one right answerKhan unit 7
Every sentence has a doing part. It says what happens.
The bee landed. Landed is the doing part.
Try it: Say the doing part: The rain stopped.
Answer: Stopped is the doing part.
4
Both parts, together one right answerKhan unit 7
A sentence needs both parts. A naming part and a doing part.
The cat slept. Cat names. Slept does.
Try it: Which part is missing? “The tall bird.”
Answer: The doing part is missing. The bird does nothing.
5
A sentence starts with a capital one right answerKhan unit 6
The first letter of a sentence is a capital.
the sky is grey. → The sky is grey.
Try it: Fix it: the pot fell.
Answer: The pot fell.
6
The full stop at the end one right answerKhan unit 6
A telling sentence ends with a full stop.
The pot fell.
Try it: Fix it: The sky is grey
Answer: The sky is grey.
7
The question mark one right answerKhan unit 6
A sentence that asks ends with a question mark.
Did the pot fall?
Try it: Turn it into an asking sentence: The sky is grey.
Answer: Is the sky grey?
8
Not a sentence yet: no naming part one right answerKhan unit 7
With no naming part, nobody knows who did it.
ran down the path → The dog ran down the path.
Try it: Add a naming part: fell off the shelf.
Answer: The dog fell off the shelf.
9
Not a sentence yet: no doing part one right answerKhan unit 7
With no doing part, nothing happens.
the small green frog → The small green frog jumped.
Try it: Add a doing part: the wet dog.
Answer: The wet dog jumped.
10
Make it longer: where one right answerKhan unit 7
You can add where it happened. In the pond, on the shelf.
The frog jumped. → The frog jumped into the pond.
Try it: Add where: The cat slept.
Answer: The cat slept on the shelf.
11
Make it longer: when one right answerKhan unit 7
You can add when it happened.
The frog jumped. → The frog jumped at night.
Try it: Add when: The cat slept.
Answer: The cat slept at night.
12
Make it longer: how her own answerKhan unit 7
You can add how it happened.
The frog jumped. → The frog jumped fast.
Try it: Add how: The cat slept.
Answer: Something like: The cat slept quietly.
13
Make it longer: a describing word her own answerKhan unit 7
A describing word tells you more about the thing.
The frog jumped. → The green frog jumped.
Try it: Add one describing word: The cat slept.
Answer: Something like: The old cat slept. Any true describing word works.
14
Join two with and one right answerKhan unit 5
And joins two things that go together.
It rained. The pot filled. → It rained and the pot filled.
Try it: Join with and: The sun came out. The path dried.
Answer: The sun came out and the path dried.
15
Join two with but one right answerKhan unit 5
But joins two things that do not match.
It rained. The soil stayed dry. → It rained but the soil stayed dry.
Try it: Join with but: The sun came out. The path stayed wet.
Answer: The sun came out but the path stayed wet.
16
Give a reason with because one right answerKhan unit 5
Because tells you why it happened.
The seed died because the soil was cold.
Try it: Finish it: The path stayed wet because…
Answer: The path stayed wet because the soil was cold.
17
Give a result with so one right answerKhan unit 5
So tells you what happened next.
The soil was cold so the seed died.
Try it: Which one is the result: “the soil was cold” or “the seed died”?
Answer: The seed died.
18
A comma before the join one right answerKhan unit 6
Put a comma before and or but. Do this when they join two whole sentences.
I planted it, and it grew.
Try it: Add the comma: The sun came out and the path dried.
Answer: The sun came out, and the path dried.
19
A comma is not enough one right answerKhan unit 7
A comma cannot hold two sentences together.
Wrong: it rained, the soil stayed dry.
Try it: Fix it with a full stop: it rained, the soil stayed dry.
Answer: It rained. The soil stayed dry.
20
Mix long and short her own answerKhan unit 7
All the same length is dull to read.
The green frog jumped into the cold pond. It sank.
Try it: Write two sentences about rain. One long, one short.
Answer: Something like: The rain fell all afternoon on the beds. Then it stopped.
21
A paragraph is about one thing one right answerKhan unit 7
A paragraph is some sentences about one thing.
Three sentences about the frog. That is one paragraph.
Try it: Does a paragraph hold one thing or many things?
Answer: One thing.
22
The first sentence one right answerKhan unit 7
Start a paragraph by saying what it is about.
The frog was hard to see.
Try it: Which first sentence is about a frog? “The frog was green.” or “I like soup.”
Answer: The frog was green.
23
The middle sentences tell more her own answerKhan unit 7
The next sentences tell more about that thing.
The frog was green. It sat on a wet leaf. It did not move.
Try it: Add one more sentence about that frog.
Answer: Something like: Then it jumped into the pond.
24
New thing, new paragraph one right answerKhan unit 7
New thing, new paragraph. Start it on a new line.
One paragraph on the frog. A new one on the pond.
Try it: You wrote about the frog. Now you write about the pond. Same paragraph or new one?
Answer: A new one.
Stage 2 Naming words and doing words lessons 25–42 · reading cap 3.1
25
A naming word is a noun one right answerKhan unit 1
A noun is a naming word. It names a person, a place or a thing.
girl, Georgia, trowel
Try it: Which one is a noun: trowel or dug?
Answer: Trowel.
26
A special name takes a capital one right answerKhan unit 1
A special name takes a capital letter.
a state → Georgia · a girl → Azianna
Try it: Which needs a capital: state or Georgia?
Answer: Georgia.
27
More than one: add s one right answerKhan unit 1
Most nouns add s to mean more than one.
seed → seeds · pot → pots · root → roots
Try it: Say more than one: pot, seed, root.
Answer: Pots, seeds, roots.
28
More than one: add es one right answerKhan unit 1
Nouns ending in s, x, ch or sh add es.
bush → bushes · box → boxes · glass → glasses
Try it: Say more than one: bush, box, glass.
Answer: Bushes, boxes, glasses.
29
More than one: y turns into ies one right answerKhan unit 1
A word ending in y takes ies.
berry → berries · fly → flies
Try it: Say more than one: berry, fly.
Answer: Berries, flies.
30
A few change all the way one right answerKhan unit 1
A few nouns change shape instead of adding s. Here they are.
child → children · foot → feet · tooth → teeth · mouse → mice · person → people
Try it: Say more than one: foot, tooth, mouse.
Answer: Feet, teeth, mice.
31
A doing word is a verb one right answerKhan unit 2
A verb is a doing word. Every sentence needs one.
She plants. It grows. They wait.
Try it: Which one is a verb: plants or trowel?
Answer: Plants.
32
Happening now one right answerKhan unit 2
A verb can say a thing is happening now.
I plant. She waits.
Try it: Say it as happening now: I planted.
Answer: I plant.
33
Already happened: add ed one right answerKhan unit 2
Most verbs add ed to say it already happened.
plant → planted · wait → waited · pull → pulled
Try it: Say it as already happened: I plant, I wait, I pull.
Answer: I planted, I waited, I pulled.
34
Verbs that do not add ed one right answerKhan unit 2
Some verbs change instead of adding ed. Here are three.
run → ran · write → wrote · grow → grew
Try it: Say it as already happened: I run, I write, I grow.
Answer: I ran, I wrote, I grew.
35
Three more that do not add ed one right answerKhan unit 2
Three more to know. They change too.
go → went · see → saw · eat → ate
Try it: Say it as already happened: I go, I see, I eat.
Answer: I went, I saw, I ate.
36
Not yet: will one right answerKhan unit 2
Will says it has not happened yet.
I will plant. She will wait.
Try it: Say it as not yet: I planted.
Answer: I will plant.
37
One grows, more than one grow one right answerKhan unit 2
One thing takes a verb with s. More than one takes no s.
The seed grows. The seeds grow.
Try it: Fix it: The seeds grows.
Answer: The seeds grow.
38
Is and are one right answerKhan unit 2
Is goes with one. Are goes with more than one.
The pot is full. The pots are full.
Try it: Fix it: The pots is full.
Answer: The pots are full.
39
Was and were one right answerKhan unit 2
Was and were say it already happened. Was for one, were for more.
The pot was full. The pots were full.
Try it: Fix it: The pots was full.
Answer: The pots were full.
40
Has and have one right answerKhan unit 2
Has goes with one. Have goes with more than one.
The plant has roots. The plants have roots.
Try it: Fix it: The plants has roots.
Answer: The plants have roots.
41
Some verbs do nothing at all one right answerKhan unit 2
Is and are do not show an action. They just say a thing is so.
The soil is dry. The leaves are green.
Try it: Which verb is in it: The soil is dry.
Answer: Is.
42
Helping words one right answerKhan unit 2
A helping word goes in front of the verb.
She is planting. She has planted.
Try it: Add a helping word: She planting.
Answer: She is planting.
Stage 3 Describing, and standing in lessons 43–58 · reading cap 3.6
43
A describing word is an adjective one right answerKhan unit 4
An adjective tells you more about a noun.
dry soil · three pots · a green leaf
Try it: Which word describes: green or leaf?
Answer: Green.
44
Pick a describing word that is true her own answerKhan unit 4
A describing word has to be true, not just nice.
Not a lovely leaf. A toothed leaf.
Try it: Describe one leaf you can see, in one true word.
Answer: Something like: a toothed leaf, or a torn leaf. True beats pretty.
45
Describing the doing one right answerKhan unit 4
Some words tell you more about the verb. Many end in ly.
She watered it slowly.
Try it: Which word tells how: slowly or watered?
Answer: Slowly.
46
Comparing two: add er one right answerKhan unit 4
Add er when you compare two things.
tall → taller · green → greener
Try it: Compare two: this plant is tall, that one is more tall.
Answer: That one is taller.
47
Comparing many: add est one right answerKhan unit 4
Add est when you compare more than two.
tall → tallest · green → greenest
Try it: Say it for many: of all my plants, this one is the most tall.
Answer: This one is the tallest.
48
Long words take more and most one right answerKhan unit 4
A long word takes more or most. Not er or est.
more careful · most careful
Try it: Fix it: she was carefuller than me.
Answer: She was more careful than me.
49
Words that stand in for a name one right answerKhan unit 3
He, she, it and they stand in for a name.
Gigi planted it. She planted it.
Try it: Which word stands in for Gigi: she or planted?
Answer: She.
50
Name first, stand-in after one right answerKhan unit 3
Say the name first. Then you can use the stand-in.
Gigi planted mint. She waters it every day.
Try it: Fix the order: She waters it. Gigi planted mint.
Answer: Gigi planted mint. She waters it.
51
Not clear? Use the name one right answerKhan unit 3
If nobody can tell who it means, put the name back.
Not clear: she told her she was late.
Try it: Make it clear: she told her she was late. One of them is Mom.
Answer: Mom told her she was late.
52
His, her, its, their one right answerKhan unit 3
These words show belonging. None of them takes an apostrophe.
her notebook · its roots · their pots
Try it: Which is right: its roots or it’s roots?
Answer: Its roots.
53
It’s means it is one right answerKhan unit 3
It’s is short for it is. Its means belonging to it.
It’s a rose. Its thorns are sharp.
Try it: Which goes here: the plant lost ___ leaves.
Answer: Its leaves.
54
Place words one right answerKhan unit 5
Some small words say where a thing is.
in, on, under, beside, behind
Try it: Pick a place word: the beetle was ___ the leaf.
Answer: Under the leaf. On, in or beside also work.
55
Time words one right answerKhan unit 5
Some small words say when a thing is.
before, after, during
Try it: Pick a time word: ___ the rain, the path dried.
Answer: After the rain, the path dried.
56
The apostrophe that shows owning one right answerKhan unit 6
Add an apostrophe and s to show it belongs to somebody.
Gigi’s notebook · the plant’s roots
Try it: Show the roots belong to the plant.
Answer: The plant’s roots.
57
Commas in a list one right answerKhan unit 6
Put a comma between things in a list.
I planted basil, mint, and thyme.
Try it: Add the commas: I planted basil mint and thyme.
Answer: I planted basil, mint, and thyme.
58
Quotation marks one right answerKhan unit 6
Quotation marks go around the words somebody said.
Gigi said, “Water it in the morning.”
Try it: Add them: Gigi said, Water it in the morning.
Answer: Gigi said, “Water it in the morning.”
Stage 4 Making the writing good lessons 59–72 · reading cap 4.1
59
Use the exact word one right answer
The exact word tells the reader more than a vague one.
Not a tool. A trowel.
Try it: Make it exact: I picked up a tool.
Answer: I picked up a trowel.
60
Say how many one right answer
A number makes what you wrote easy to believe.
Not lots of ants. Eleven ants.
Try it: Add a number: I saw lots of ants.
Answer: I saw eleven ants.
61
Compare it to something her own answer
A comparison helps the reader see it.
The seed was the size of a full stop.
Try it: Compare one thing you saw today to something in the house.
Answer: Something like: the leaf was as wide as my hand.
62
Show it, do not name it one right answer
Instead of naming a feeling, say what you saw.
Not it was sad. The leaves had gone grey and soft.
Try it: Show it: the plant was sad.
Answer: The leaves had gone grey and soft.
63
Start where something happens one right answer
Begin at the moment, not at the introduction.
Not I am going to write about a beetle. The beetle was under the leaf.
Try it: Cut the slow start: I am going to write about a beetle. The beetle was under the leaf.
Answer: The beetle was under the leaf.
64
Write what you saw her own answer
Write what is there, not what you think should be there.
The bud did not open. That is worth writing down.
Try it: Write one thing that did not go the way you expected.
Answer: Something like: the bud did not open, even after a week.
65
Read it out loud her own answer
Read it out loud. Anywhere you stumble is a sentence to change.
If you run out of breath, the sentence is too long.
Try it: Read what you wrote out loud and find one bumpy sentence.
Answer: Any sentence you stumbled on. Cut it in two and read it again.
66
Cut the words that do nothing one right answer
Very, really and quite add nothing. Cut them.
the very really green leaf → the green leaf
Try it: Cut it down: the quite very tall plant.
Answer: The tall plant.
67
Do not repeat a word one right answer
The same word twice in two sentences makes writing flat.
The leaf was green. The stem was green too. → The stem was green too.
Try it: Fix it: I saw a bee. I saw a fly.
Answer: I saw a bee, and then a fly.
68
One idea to a paragraph one right answer
When you change idea, start a new paragraph.
What it looked like. Then, on its own, what it did.
Try it: You wrote what the bee looked like. Now what it did. New paragraph?
Answer: Yes. New idea, new paragraph.
69
Answer what you were asked her own answer
Before you finish, read the prompt again.
Half of writing well is answering the actual question.
Try it: Read today’s prompt again. Did you answer it?
Answer: If not, add one sentence that does. That is the whole fix.
70
Check the capitals and the full stops her own answer
Every sentence starts with a capital and ends with a full stop.
the pot fell → The pot fell.
Try it: Check every sentence you wrote today for both.
Answer: Fix any that are missing. That is the check.
71
Check both parts are there her own answer
Every sentence needs a naming part and a doing part.
Not a sentence: running through the garden.
Try it: Find one sentence you wrote and name both parts.
Answer: If one part is missing, add it. Then it is a sentence.
72
Stop when you are done one right answer
When you have said it, stop. Do not add a big ending.
Not And that is what I learned about beetles.
Try it: Which is the better last line: “It flew off.” or “And that is my beetle report.”
Answer: It flew off.
